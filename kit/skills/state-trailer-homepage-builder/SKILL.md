# State-Focused Trailer Rental Homepage Builder

## Role and objective
Act as a senior American B2B website architect, technical SEO specialist, local search strategist, conversion specialist, and natural commercial copywriter. Build **only a new production-ready homepage** for a trailer rental business. Serve real buyers first while meeting Kermit's measurable SEO standards. Avoid AI-sounding filler, keyword stuffing, and fabricated claims.

## Project inputs
- Brand: `[BRAND_NAME]`
- Production domain: `[DOMAIN]`
- Target state: `[STATE_NAME]`
- Primary rental service: `[PRIMARY_RENTAL_SERVICE]`
- Local asset folder: `[LOCAL_ASSET_FOLDER]`
- Repository: `[PROJECT_REPOSITORY]`
- Verified phone/email: `[PHONE]` / `[EMAIL]`
- Optional approved Google Drive equipment folder: https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1vQ4YVHz5H619Y1UqtX18vL0MQBiUHR73

Never hardcode a particular state. Ask if the primary service cannot be determined from verified business information.

## Inventory and asset verification
Prefer the accessible **local asset folder** for speed, then authorized Google Drive access, then existing repository assets and verified business documents. Do not assume Google Drive access. Catalog each trailer's category, image source, specifications, rental availability, and missing facts. Do not assume an image in the shared folder proves the business owns or rents the equipment. Use real, licensed equipment photography; never fabricate inventory or specs.

## Strict scope and navigation
Build the **homepage only**. Do not create equipment, service-area, use-case, about, contact, city, or other interior pages. Navigation labels and order must be exactly:

**Home | Equipment | Service Area | Use cases | About Us | Contact Us**

Do not add other menu labels. The header may have a separate **Request Quote** button. Link to verified existing routes only; otherwise document pending destinations without creating broken links. Homepage summaries of these subjects are permitted, but do not build their standalone page content.

## State-first SEO and keyword strategy
- Select one primary commercial keyword: `[PRIMARY_RENTAL_SERVICE] + [STATE_NAME]`.
- Use it naturally in the SEO title, H1, opening paragraph, and relevant sections; vary phrasing where helpful.
- Use related terms such as mobile, portable, temporary, emergency, rental, for rent, and lease **only when accurate and intent-relevant**.
- Keep the main rental family dominant. Related equipment must support the primary topic.
- Make the target state and relevant municipalities central to the homepage. Do not dilute a low-authority state site with nationwide keyword lists.
- Kermit's low-DA state strategy is a company convention, not a Google ranking requirement.

## Compact Keywords integration — purchase-intent SEO
Combine Kermit's state/municipality standards with the publicly described principles of Edward Sturm's Compact Keywords approach: prioritize searchers who already need a service, clear offers, specific scenarios, and a direct next action. Reference: https://edwardsturm.com/compact-keywords/ . This skill does not claim to reproduce private course materials.

### Buyer-intent keyword research before writing
- Build a short keyword decision matrix for the **homepage only** with candidate query, intent, relevant verified equipment, geographic fit, likely buyer, competition evidence, and proposed page placement.
- Start with `[PRIMARY_RENTAL_SERVICE] rental [STATE_NAME]` and evaluate natural alternatives: `[SERVICE] for rent [STATE]`, `[SERVICE] for lease [STATE]`, `emergency [SERVICE] rental [STATE]`, and `[SERVICE] rental near [MUNICIPALITY]`. Include a variant only when it matches real inventory, service availability, and natural search language.
- Check actual search results and available GSC/keyword tools to distinguish transactional intent from informational intent. Do not invent search volume, CPC, keyword difficulty, ranking potential, or conversion data. Mark unknowns.
- Prefer specific buying scenarios: restaurant kitchen closed for renovations, hospital foodservice continuity, temporary refrigeration after equipment failure, event catering capacity, or a remote workforce feeding operation. Only use scenarios appropriate to the verified equipment.
- Choose **one dominant homepage intent**. Group close variants on the same homepage when they satisfy the same buyer need; do not create separate near-duplicate sections for each synonym. Distinct equipment families and detailed municipality searches may deserve future pages, but **do not build those pages now**.
- Prioritize usefulness over arbitrary word count: Kermit's municipality and word-count guidelines are editorial checks, not a reason to add filler. Every section should answer a buyer's decision question.

### Conversion-focused content pattern
For each major homepage section, answer: **Who needs this? What can they rent? Where is it relevant? What evidence supports it? What is the next step?** Use concise, direct copy and a clear quote/availability action at natural decision points. Provide equipment dimensions, utilities, rental duration, pricing, delivery limitations, and emergency response details only when verified.

### Keyword placement and measurement
- Place the strongest state + rental term in the H1/title; include helpful natural variations in equipment headings, city examples, pricing and FAQs where relevant.
- Do not cram multiple city names and near-identical service variants into the hero, footer, alt text or hidden content.
- Report a post-build keyword matrix: target term, buyer intent, placement, supporting evidence, and CTA destination. Record any overlap with planned interior pages as future architecture only.
- Measure success using qualified quote requests, phone clicks, form completion, organic landing traffic, and eventual booked rentals when tracking exists. Never promise rankings, leads or sales.

## Municipality rules
For roughly 1,400–2,000 words, plan approximately 10–14 distinct relevant municipalities, adjusted to genuine service coverage and evidence. Use approximately one distinct municipality per 80–170 words as an **editorial guideline**, not a mandatory density target. Feature two anchor municipalities in deeper practical examples. Smaller municipalities (roughly 300–10,000 residents) may be useful under Kermit's strategy when genuine rental demand and verified population justify them.

Keep incorporated cities/towns separate from counties, states, regions, metros, districts, and unincorporated communities. Tie each location mention to meaningful customer needs: renovations, institutions, emergency operations, delivery access, utilities, seasonal conditions, or actual projects. Never imply a hypothetical scenario is a completed job.

## Visual design standards
Build a clean modern B2B design: spacious typography, strong authentic trailer photography, restrained branding, responsive grids, accessible CTAs, and meaningful visual hierarchy. Avoid generic gradients, glowing effects, repetitive card walls, overcrowded hero copy, intrusive overlays, fake reviews, and filler. Optimize desktop, tablet, and mobile separately.

## Required homepage wireframe — 15 sections

### 01. Header and navigation
Logo, exact six navigation labels, and separate Request Quote CTA. Compact sticky behavior only if it does not obscure content. Accessible mobile menu.

### 02. Hero and primary CTA
Two-column desktop composition: state-specific eyebrow, **one H1**, 50–90-word natural commercial description, primary quote CTA, optional emergency contact CTA, and real primary trailer photo with verified caption. State, service, and buyer action must be obvious immediately. Avoid long city lists in the hero.

### 03. Quick rental inquiry
Equipment type, city/ZIP, start date, duration, and contact details. Validate inputs and use a real approved submission destination. Never pretend a mock form works.

### 04. Equipment showcase
Real photos, clear names, practical benefits, verified specs, and links only to existing relevant destinations. Visually emphasize the primary rental family.

### 05. Why choose us
Verified operational advantages: equipment fit, utility planning, rental coordination, site preparation, and delivery logistics. No invented experience, guarantees, certifications, or response times.

### 06. Customer testimonials
Genuine attributed reviews and verified ratings only. Accessible carousel if useful. If reviews are unavailable, omit fabricated cards and report the missing evidence.

### 07. Municipality rental applications
Short, concrete examples connecting municipality, customer, operational problem, suitable equipment, and site logistics. Distinguish verified projects from hypothetical scenarios.

### 08. Interactive state map
Accurate lightweight SVG outline of `[STATE_NAME]`, **not a USA map**. Place correctly positioned municipality markers for verified coverage; support keyboard use and mobile viewing. Provide a crawlable HTML city list beside or below the map. Link only to existing city pages; do not fabricate routes or imply guaranteed delivery.

### 09. Rental pricing and availability
Show verified rates or ranges, location applicability, rental period, minimum terms, delivery, collection, setup, and exclusions. If prices are unavailable, clearly say **Request a quote**. Never invent numbers.

### 10. Industries and use cases
Short, relevant summaries for actual applications such as restaurant renovation, hospitals, schools, construction, emergency foodservice, and remote workforce projects, when equipment supports them. Do not create interior pages.

### 11. Emergency rental support
Explain urgent inquiries and the information required: location, capacity, utilities, access, and timeline. Refrigeration-focused homepages should strongly emphasize credible emergency assistance. Advertise 24/7 only if verified; do not promise immediate dispatch or availability without evidence.

### 12. How rental works
Four clear steps: inquire, select suitable equipment, review site requirements, confirm quote and delivery. Match the actual workflow.

### 13. Technical planning guide
Concise and useful information on footprint, power, water, drainage, ventilation, capacity, access, and placement. Numerical specs must be verified.

### 14. Local FAQs
Direct answers to genuine state-specific buyer questions about pricing, delivery, rental terms, utilities, emergency availability, and equipment suitability. Avoid repetitive keyword answers.

### 15. Final CTA and footer
Clear quote request asking for location, equipment, rental period, and requirements. Verified contact details. Footer uses the same six navigation labels; no extra menu categories.

## Internal links and architecture
Homepage is the commercial hub. Link naturally to **existing** equipment, service-area, use-case, and contact destinations. Do not generate those pages in this task. Avoid fake URLs, exact-match anchor repetition, or links that return 404.

## Local knowledge and factual integrity
Research relevant cities, hospitals, schools, employers, venues, industry, seasonal weather, transportation, emergency history, and delivery constraints using credible evidence. Separate (1) verified business projects, (2) verified local facts, and (3) illustrative rental scenarios. Never imply an institution is a customer without proof. Never fabricate dispatch, inventory, photographs, emergency-response projects, or local relationships.

## Technical SEO and QA
Implement one H1, logical H2/H3, unique title/meta description, canonical, appropriate robots directives, alt text, crawlable links, valid fact-supported schema, responsive images, accessibility, and Core Web Vitals optimization. Test forms, navigation, breakpoints, and all assets. Avoid duplicate city templates, hidden keyword blocks, broken routes, and unsupported review schema.

## Kermit's 18-point final audit
Provide a separate, evidence-based report after building:

1. **Most-mentioned cities:** top two with exact counts; report ties.
2. **Total cities:** list every unique city and its count, total mentions; separate other geographic types. Define counting method on rendered visible content.
3. **Rental subjects:** main service frequency, related service/trailer frequencies, and meaningful municipality connections.
4. **Content quality:** 1–10 for clarity, usefulness, naturalness, and repetition; strengths, weaknesses, approximate word count.
5. **Worker time:** realistic writing, formatting, imagery, and QA estimates for template vs new design.
6. **Competitor:** compare with https://thelavatoryfresno.com/ on clarity, usefulness, naturalness, specific rentals, locations, and repetition; score both fairly.
7. **Audit identification:** production landing-page URL and worker name.
8. **Local knowledge:** verify landmarks, organizations, delivery logistics, emergencies, events, industry, climate, dispatch, photos, and credible evidence.
9. **Local evidence comparison:** score both pages 1–10; target at least 7.5 by improving real usefulness, **never by inventing proof**. Separate actual projects from examples.
10. **State-focused homepage:** verify state focus in heading, introduction, services, and cities; check interior-page backlink where one exists; include clickable homepage URL and score.
11. **24/7 and emergency:** emergency emphasis 1–10 (target 8–10 for refrigeration; at least 4 for others where relevant), plus separate clarity, usefulness, and credibility. Verify 24/7.
12. **Reserved:** leave pending; do not invent a replacement.
13. **Pricing:** service, location, price, period, delivery, collection, setup, minimums, exclusions; verified or explicitly estimated only; transparency score.
14. **Authority:** current production-domain Moz DA and/or Ahrefs DR, provider and date; unknown if inaccessible. Never substitute Vercel authority or equate DA and DR.
15. **CTAs:** visibility, usefulness, destination, consistent contact information, and mobile testing; score.
16. **Internal links:** relevance, descriptive anchors, homepage/location/service/contact paths, verified broken links; score.
17. **Municipalities and coverage:** classify cities, towns, counties, states, regions, metros, districts, neighborhoods, and unincorporated areas; explain meaningful geographic coverage and include in local-evidence scoring.
18. **Low-DA focus:** when verified Moz DA <=10, score state and municipality homepage focus; if DA unknown, mark threshold unverified. Company strategy, not Google policy.

## Completion criteria
Verify all business claims and equipment imagery; check spelling and municipality classification; validate map geometry and markers; test buttons and real form submission; inspect desktop/tablet/mobile; ensure no overlap or clipping; confirm **only homepage** was created and the menu labels are exact. Run the 18-point audit and report unresolved information honestly. A successful build alone is not completion.
